AI has a firm grasp on me. My emotions on it seem to shift all over the place, so I'm sometimes confused.
Thats why I'm writing this list. Both good and bad.

## Things I'm 100% sure of

- I absolutely hate realizing I've been fooled by AI content. It feels like betrayal when the piece of content I gave my time and attention to, wasn't given time and attention by person that generated it. And no, "it took a lot of prompting to get it right" doesn't count. I know I'm not the only one who feels like this.
- I'm sure I've also been fooled without ever finding out.
- I'm a web developer. My job has completely changed and there's no going back. I'm a prompter now. And I feel no pride in my output any more. Nothing's preventing me from writing code in my spare time (which I'll still do because I like it), but economically it's no longer viable. So I'm grieving for my career.
- There's a nagging unbalance in web development now. Sometimes AI enables us to finish a task so quickly that we're ashamed to deploy it straight away. At the same time, sometimes a task actually does take long but a client will ask "doesn't AI make this trivial?".
- I'm happy for my friends and colleagues with almost-complete side projects who could finish them because of AI. More often than not, they were left unfinished.
- Humans will always feel pride in doing hard things. While AI makes it easier to not do hard things, self-actualization is at the top of Maslow's pyramid for a reason.


## Things I'm pretty certain of
- I think I'm better than average in recognizing AI content. I also think the majority of people thinks they're better than average at recognizing AI content.
- Web developers will become obselete sooner rather than later. Most people in my environment arent as outspoken on it as I am, but I think they're being naive. They celebrate their little victories over AI ("I'd like to see AI do THAT!") but they're constantly shifting the goal posts ("Okay AI can now X this better than I can, but it still can't do Y!"). 
- Almost fully automating something feels worse than fully automating and fully doing it manual. Take autonomous driving for instance. Current laws enforce you to still pay attention. European cars are required to have sensor that annoy you when you take your hands off the wheel for too long. I don't mind manually driving. I like being driven somewhere. But not driving while also being forced to pay attention? That sucks. I feel like this is a good analogy for my career now.

## Things I'm afraid of
- AI will multiply the effects of capitalism. Big companies spent trillions of dollars investing in AI and they'll want to earn it back one way or another. Either the bubble bursts and the government will bail the big companies out, or the wealth shifts even more towards the ultra-rich. Either way, the divide between rich and poor gets bigger.
- It'll be the death of personal computing. All compute resources are being bought up and being rented back to the consumer at a premium. Everything is a subscription now and they'll only get more expensive.
- I'll have to change my career. Because my skill has become a commodity and because I don't want to prompt for a career. Double whammy.

## Things I'm hopefull of
- We'll get etiquettes about AI usage. Right now it feels like people are feeling smart to use AI to avoid work, but don't yet realize the effect it has on other people. Even for mundane stuff, like soccer score predictions. Yes, your predictions will be more accurate because of it and you can easily get away with it, but why would you? It's like using your phone to cheat in a pub quiz, it's silly and not done.
- Since AI, I've written a lot more (this article for example). I've also started digitally painting. I did both almost out of protest because I wanted to do hard things again and because I want more self expression, not less. Ironically, pushed me in that direction. The better AI becomes, the more I value imperfect things such as my own art.

## Things I'm unsure about
- I read a quote somewhere: "With so much generated code, the complexity of a system can grow exponentially. It can be so complex that the only way to make sense of it is to use an LLM. Developers, teams, whole companies can become addicted and dependent on generative AI.". I think that's true, but I honestly don't know if that's a bad thing. The better the autopilot the worse the pilot. But is that bad? I genuinely don't know. Maybe I should read Joel's 'leaky abstractions' article again.
