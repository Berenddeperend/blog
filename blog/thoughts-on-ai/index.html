Thoughts on AI

Posted on Jun 2, 2026

AI is taking my job. Since around december 2025, Claude has become good enough that it's no longer viable to write code by hand. Naturally I started worrying about being replaced by AI. Now, six months later, a new problem arose: I hate what AI turned my job into. I'm a prompter now. I spent ten years getting good at what I do, and that skill has now lost much of its meaning.

I'm proud of my skill to write code. I love losing myself in 'the zone', where the hours fly by because I'm working at the intersection of challenge, skill, understanding and opportunity. Now, code is no longer my output. My output is a prompt. A prompt that anyone could have written. Sure, it helps a bit to know what to ask and to steer AI in the right direction, but with the speed AI has been progressing, it's naive to think that will keep us developers employed. And the work that remains is just so boring. The optimal way to do my job is to ask AI and wait for it to be done. Any mistakes it makes are one prompt removed from being fixed. I prompted an app that's better than I could ever code by hand, but felt no pride. I no longer need to collaborate with colleagues. My days never felt longer. I'm not willing to do this anymore.

So AI made me give up my career. And I'm grieving for my craft. But honestly, I'm sort of fine with this outcome. Before AI, developers made countless jobs obsolete. It's a poetic justice that developers are meeting the same fate. I got ten good years of employment out of programming. I'm grateful for that.

What I'm not okay with though, is how AI has changed human connection.

I felt deeply insulted when I realized on day three of a friend's Polarsteps journey, that the words I read were not theirs. I hate that I can no longer tell if images or videos are real or fake. I'm tired of being polite when someone wants to show me their generated music. I get discouraged thinking about how we'll talk the way AI talks because of all the exposure we get to it.
I get worried when I imagine a future where society gets even more divided because evidence for made up facts can be generated.

I'm fine with AI replacing code. Code is a tool, AI is a tool. I'm not fine with AI replacing humans expressing themselves. And I know I'm not the only one feeling this way. I know there will be a renaissance where we value imperfect genuinity. I don't know how yet, but I'd love to contribute to this renaissance. Please do get in touch if you'd like to connect and share thoughts.
